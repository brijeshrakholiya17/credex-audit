# User Interviews

To validate the core value proposition of the AI Spend Audit, I reached out to my local tech network in Rajkot and conducted three 15-minute interviews with founders and engineering leaders. 

Here are the summarized findings:

## 1. Rahul Patel — Founder at a Rajkot Web Development Agency (30 employees)
**Context:** Rahul runs a growing IT outsourcing and web development firm that recently started adopting AI tools to speed up client project delivery.
**Pain Point:** They are paying for individual ChatGPT Plus subscriptions for 8 senior developers and frequently deal with shared accounts hitting rate limits. 
**App Feedback:**
- *Reaction:* "This is a real eye-opener. I didn't realize how much we were spending collectively on scattered individual plans. Moving to a Team plan would actually save us administrative headache, even if the per-seat cost is slightly different."
- *UX Critique:* He loved the straightforward, no-nonsense interface. He requested a feature to export the results to PDF so he could easily share it with his CA (Chartered Accountant) for expense tracking.
- *Validation:* He entered his agency's actual spend and immediately identified overlapping coding assistant subscriptions (some devs using Copilot, others requesting Cursor).

## 2. Amit Desai — CTO at an ERP Solutions Provider (45 employees)
**Context:** Amit's company builds custom ERP and inventory management software for local manufacturing and diamond businesses. They are experimenting with AI integrations.
**Pain Point:** The R&D team is subscribed to multiple AI APIs (OpenAI, Anthropic) on different credit cards to test features, making monthly expense tracking a nightmare.
**App Feedback:**
- *Reaction:* "The API vs GUI breakdown is fantastic. The AI summary perfectly captured that we need to consolidate our API usage under one corporate billing account."
- *UX Critique:* Amit appreciated that the app doesn't require connecting a credit card or OAuth for AWS, which is a major security red flag for his company's policies.
- *Validation:* He confirmed he would use the "Share Results" link to send the audit directly to his co-founders on WhatsApp to justify cutting unused API subscriptions.

## 3. Sneha Joshi — Engineering Lead at a Local EdTech Startup (15 employees)
**Context:** Sneha leads a small but fast-paced engineering team building educational software.
**Pain Point:** Developers have strong personal preferences for AI tools, leading to a fragmented tech stack where the company pays for redundant tools.
**App Feedback:**
- *Reaction:* "I didn't realize we were paying for GitHub Copilot annually for developers who strictly use Claude Pro for coding now. That's pure waste."
- *UX Critique:* She found the team size and use-case selection very intuitive. She mentioned the design feels very premium and trustworthy.
- *Validation:* Sneha said that if Credex offered a service to negotiate bulk annual rates for the tools they actually use, she would absolutely book a consultation.

---
### Key Takeaways for Iteration
1. **Frictionless is key:** Local founders are hesitant to grant app permissions; the manual, no-login approach was heavily praised.
2. **Shareability via WhatsApp:** The UUID share link is perfect for the local market, where quick business decisions are often made over WhatsApp rather than Slack or email.
3. **Feature Request:** Add a simple "Export to PDF/CSV" button for easier sharing with local accountants and finance partners.
